Live-update atoms

The small chrome that signals real-time without overwhelming. Pulse on what's live, not on what's important. Use one indicator per region — a page covered in pulsing dots reads as broken.

Rules

  1. Pick the skeleton from the user's task. KPI for "what's the headline," ops for "where's the problem," split-pane for "browse-and-detail," table-first for "show me everything." Mixing skeletons on one page = confused users.
  2. One density per surface, set on the page-shell or section. Don't mix compact and comfortable in the same view — it reads as sloppy.
  3. KPI cards: cap at 4–6. Beyond that they become wallpaper. Promote the most important to an explicit headline (chart with big number).
  4. Filter bar is part of the table, not a separate widget. Same width, sits directly above. Search input goes on the right, chips on the left.
  5. Sticky header on any table that scrolls. Sticky first column when the row identifier is long enough to lose context.
  6. Group rows are uppercase mini-headers, not full table rows. They reset the eye between sections.
  7. Footer = totals, not "see more." The user wants the sum.
  8. "Last updated" lives in the topbar, right side, mono, secondary color. The pulse goes on the data region itself, not on the timestamp.
  9. No more than one "live" pulse per region. Two competing pulses create the impression of multiple things needing attention.
  10. Empty toolbar > clutter. If there are no actions yet, leave the action area blank. Don't fill it with a "tip" or a logo.

Anti-patterns

  • 12 KPI cards in two rows. Reading suspended. Pick four. Promote the others to a "all metrics" page.
  • Both a sidebar nav and a top tab bar. Pick one. Two navs = the page has no spine.
  • Filters that don't restate selection in the table header. If the user filters to "failed runs," the table title should change to reflect it. Otherwise they forget what they're looking at.
  • Spinning loader on a 300ms response. Use a disabled state on the button instead. Skeletons are for >1s; spinners are almost never the right answer.
  • Pie charts on dashboards. See charts — bar is almost always better. Pie is permitted at n ≤ 3 and only when the parts-of-whole reading is the entire point.
